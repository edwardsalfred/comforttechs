Patterns of the top 10%

1. Persistent sticky CTA bar

Phone + "Request Service" never leaves the viewport. Honeycomb, Cowboys, Champion, Beyer all do this. Comfort Techs does not.

2. Hero offer above the fold

A tangible price hook ($58 tune-up at Honeycomb, free estimate at Cowboys). Not "Welcome to our website." Comfort Techs has $8,888 system replacement and $89/mo financing, both stronger than competitor offers, both currently buried.

3. Real techs in real photos

Honeycomb and Cowboys lead with named technicians on the homepage. Trust forms in 0.5 seconds. Comfort Techs has Isidro and a six-person team already photographed. They belong on the homepage, not buried on /team.

4. Trust stack visible early

Star count, BBB, OEM badges (Trane / Daikin / Amana), local press. Top performers stack 4 to 6 trust signals near the hero. Comfort Techs has Amana Newcomer 2024 + 2025, NATE, TACCA, Daikin Comfort Pro, currently invisible above the fold.

5. Two-track CTA

Phone for urgency callers, online form for browsers. Top sites accommodate both. The current Comfort Techs site has the call link but the form path is friction-heavy.

Recommended design direction

Color palette. Deep HVAC blue (#0B3D91) + bright sky (#0E72D6) + warm orange CTA (#FF6A2B). Avoids competitor red (Cowboys), honey (Honeycomb), brown (Champion), and corporate light blue (Beyer).

Typography. Inter for the workhorse. Instrument Serif for editorial accents. Humanizes the brand and visibly separates Comfort Techs from sans-only competitors.

Photography. Lead with Isidro's portrait + branded van. Deprecate generic Hibu hero. Treat existing equipment shots with overlay gradient for cohesion.

Animation. GSAP + ScrollTrigger choreography. Every named competitor is 100% static. A single layer of motion is an instant differentiator at this price tier, used sparingly so the page never feels gimmicky.

Structure. Single long-scroll homepage. Sticky nav. Twelve services in a card grid. Specials, founder story, team grid, service area, final 24/7 CTA. Conversion-first.
